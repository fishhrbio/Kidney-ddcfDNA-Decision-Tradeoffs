About this project. This educational companion to a narrative review on kidney-transplant dd-cfDNA interpretation explores a practical question: how does combining fractional and absolute measurements change diagnostic errors?

The evidence comes from Nie et al. (2025). The guide translates published summary statistics into a comparison of four rules, with explanations of their biological context and limits.

03 / Connect the arithmetic to biology

Why two measurements can disagree

Fraction and concentration have different denominators

Fraction is donor-derived cfDNA as a percentage of total cfDNA. Recipient-derived cfDNA can change the total denominator. A changing fraction does not necessarily imply proportional change in donor-derived concentration.

In this study, donor fraction came from a targeted 441-SNP sequencing assay; total cfDNA was quantified by qPCR. Absolute dd-cfDNA was calculated as total cfDNA × donor fraction (as a proportion). The two readouts are mathematically linked.

Mathematical coupling is not mechanistic proof

Calibration, collection and processing, non-rejection injury, and phenotype composition can contribute to discordance. The published summaries do not identify a patient's mechanism. Dividing group medians does not recover patient-level total cfDNA.

Who counts as “rejection” matters

The source defines 14 ABMR/mixed cases and 1 TCMR case as rejection. Its 35 comparators include 6 borderline TCMR, 4 BK virus nephropathy (BKVN), 12 IgAN/FSGS, and 13 other cases. Comparator does not mean healthy graft. With a single TCMR case, generalization across rejection phenotypes is limited.

100% observed sensitivity does not mean certainty

OR detected 15/15 rejection cases and had 23/23 true negatives among its negative results. Small denominators, cutoffs selected in the same cohort, and absent external validation remain important. These results do not demonstrate that a test can safely replace biopsy.

04 / Understand the result

What this analysis accomplishes

  1. Makes errors tangible. Percentages become counts: AND avoids two false positives but misses three additional rejections compared with fraction alone.
  2. Checks the arithmetic. Uniquely reconstructed counts reproduce all 16 reported rates, satisfy AND/OR set relationships, and remain unchanged across three rounding tolerances.
  3. Connects measurement to biology. Fraction and absolute concentration provide related views of donor-derived DNA; agreement does not make either a direct explanation of injury.
  4. Defines the evidence boundary. One small cohort with study-selected cutoffs cannot establish a universal rule or replace independent clinical validation.
